Lab 4: HMWK 4 — Random walks, LLN and CLT

n systems and m attackers: simulate Bernoulli and Rademacher penetration scores and compare S_n, S_n/n and S_n/√n against the Law of Large Numbers and the Central Limit Theorem.

1. Model

There are n systems and m attackers. Each attacker works through the systems one after the other and scores Xi on the i-th one; the increments are i.i.d. and the running total Sk = X1 + … + Xk is a random walk. The m attackers are independent trajectories of the same process, so the m values of Sn are a sample of its distribution.

3. Variance under the three normalizations

Empirical sample variance over the m attackers / theoretical value.

ModelnVar[Sn]Var[Sn/n]Var[Sn/√n]
  • Sn: variance grows linearly (nσ²); the totals of different attackers drift further apart.
  • Sn/n: variance shrinks like σ²/n; dividing by n is too strong and everything concentrates.
  • Sn/√n: variance stays at σ². The scale √n is the only one that gives a non-degenerate limit.

4. Law of Large Numbers

5. Central Limit Theorem

If μ = 0 (Rademacher), Sn/√n → N(0, 1) in distribution. For a general model the statement applies to (Sn − nμ)/(σ√n). The table gives the mean of Sn/√n and the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance between the standardized sample and N(0,1).

Modelnmean of Sn/√nKS distance (standardized)
  • For n = 10 the Rademacher histogram is visibly discrete (11 possible values) and the KS distance is large: the normal is only an approximation.
  • The KS distance shrinks as n grows until it reaches the noise floor of a finite sample, about 1/√m (5% critical value for exact N(0,1): ). Beyond that, larger n cannot be told apart from the limit with this m; a larger m is needed.
  • Bernoulli with p ≠ ½ is skewed for small n; the CLT still holds, but convergence is slower the further p is from ½.
  • Without centering, Sn/√n is not tight when μ ≠ 0: its mean √n·p diverges, as the table shows for Bernoulli. LLN and CLT describe different scales: the first is the centre, the second the fluctuation around it.

6. Interpretation: systems and attackers

  • One attacker, n systems. Sn is the attacker's cumulative penetration score. With Bernoulli, it counts compromised systems, with mean np; the path is a noisy line of slope p. With Rademacher, successes and setbacks cancel and the attacker's total wanders around 0 with typical size √n.
  • Effect of the system size n. Larger n makes the fraction of compromised systems Sn/n predictable (variance p(1−p)/n), while the count becomes less predictable in absolute terms (standard deviation σ√n). Large infrastructures behave in aggregate like their average; the risk of a given attacker's total is not small in absolute terms.
  • Effect of the number of attackers m. Attackers are independent replicas, so m does not change the distribution of any single Sn. It controls how well we can estimate it: the error of an empirical mean or probability scales like 1/√m, and the histogram and KS distance become sharper. Pooling all attackers gives mn trials with total mean mnμ and variance mnσ², so the same LLN and CLT apply to the aggregate score.
  • Symmetric case. In the zero-mean model no attacker has a systematic advantage; any large positive total is a fluctuation, of order σ√n, which the normal law quantifies (about 95% of totals lie within ±2√n).
  • Assumption. The conclusions rely on independence and identical distribution of the systems. Correlated systems (shared credentials, common software) increase the variance beyond nσ² and can slow or break the √n scaling.
